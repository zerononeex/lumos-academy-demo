Security & privacy for schools

Last updated 23 October 2026. A summary for teachers and district privacy officers. Districts receive the full Privacy Impact Assessment support pack, threat model and test results on request.

Status: Moonbeam Academy is pre-pilot and not yet deployed. No real student data is held. The controls below are built and tested in the code unless marked planned.

Hosting region

Planned (pending owner approval): Microsoft Azure Canada Central (Toronto), with geo-redundant backups in Canada East (Quebec City). Production data, backups and logs are planned to stay in Canada, and production administrative access is planned to be limited to Canada-based staff. BC law no longer requires Canadian storage; it is a choice we make for districts.

Encryption

  • In transit: HTTPS only; browsers are told to always use HTTPS (Strict-Transport-Security). TLS is planned to terminate in Canada (no global CDN in front of the app).
  • At rest: planned Azure-managed encryption of the database, backups and storage, with customer-managed keys as a pilot option.
  • Adult passwords are stored only as Argon2id hashes. Children's session tokens, invite and password-reset links, and two-step sign-in recovery codes are stored only as hashes; a child's secret login picture is stored only as a keyed hash. Adult session tokens are stored in a database area that only the sign-in service can read.

Adult accounts

  • No self sign-up. Staff are invited by a district administrator. Parents and guardians join only by an invite from their child's teacher or a district administrator, one child per invite. Invite and reset links work once and expire (48 hours and 30 minutes).
  • Passwords need at least 12 characters. Passwords found in known data breaches are refused; the check sends only the first 5 characters of the password's SHA-1 hash to the Have I Been Pwned range service, never the password.
  • Two-step sign-in (an authenticator app) is required for district administrators, recommended for teachers and optional for parents. Recovery codes work once.
  • Sessions end after 30 minutes of inactivity and after 8 hours in any case. Signing out, changing a password, a role change and deactivation end sessions immediately. Sign-in, reset and invite attempts are rate-limited per account and per network address, with the same answer whether or not an account exists.
  • Bulk exports, role changes, deactivation, deletion and security settings ask for the password (and code) again.

Tenant isolation

Each district is a separate tenant. Isolation is enforced in the database itself with PostgreSQL row-level security, enabled and forced on every table that holds district data, so a bug in one page cannot show another district's rows. The application runs as database roles that cannot bypass those rules, and every request is checked again in the application (role and relationship). Automated tests try cross-district reads, writes and guessed IDs on every build.

  • Teachers see only their own classes. Parents and guardians see only their own linked children. District administrators manage staff, rosters and consent records but cannot see children's learning data, except through a logged, time-limited “break-glass” access with a stated reason that every administrator in the district can see. No staff member sees learning data for a child without a consent record.
  • Every adult sign-in, failed sign-in, view or export of a child's data, role or link change, break-glass use and deletion is written to an audit log that cannot be edited or deleted by the app. Bulk exports and unusual activity are shown to district administrators.
  • Children log in with a class code (open only while the teacher allows), their name, and a secret picture; login is rate-limited and locks after repeated wrong pictures. Sessions are short and end with “I'm done”, which wipes the shared device.
  • Games run in a sandbox with no network access and never receive a child's name or the app's cookies. Scores are computed by the server, never trusted from the device.
  • Strict Content-Security-Policy with no third-party origins; no analytics or trackers; essential cookies only (list).

Retention and deletion

Practice records are kept for the current school year plus one; a district can delete any student at any time and receives a deletion receipt; deletion reaches backups within 35 days. At the end of a contract all of a district's data is deleted and certified. Details: Privacy Policy.

Subprocessors

Planned: Microsoft Azure (hosting, Canada Central, with geo-redundant backups in Canada East). Roadmap, only with the district's assessment: Microsoft Entra ID (district single sign-on). Used for the breached-password check (receives only a 5-character hash prefix, no personal data): Have I Been Pwned range service. Email delivery for invites and password resets (planned, not set up): Microsoft Azure Communication Services, Canada data location to be verified at setup. Not used: analytics, advertising, session replay, third-party fonts or scripts, AI services.

If something goes wrong

We notify an affected district within 24 hours of confirming a breach involving its students, with what happened, what data, which students (by pseudonymous record) and what we have done, followed by a full report within 72 hours; we keep a register of every breach for at least 24 months and report to the Privacy Commissioner of Canada where the law requires.

Assurance roadmap

Our controls are designed to align with SOC 2. We do not hold a SOC 2 report or any security certification, and no independent penetration test has been completed yet. An independent penetration test is required before the pilot uses real student data; a SOC 2 audit is a gate before scaling beyond pilots.
