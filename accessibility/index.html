Accessibility Statement

Last updated 23 October 2026.

Our target

We aim for WCAG 2.2 Level AA on everything children and teachers use: login, the student launcher, the game frame and its games, the teacher pages, and these public pages. Moonbeam Academy is partially conformant: we meet many AA criteria and have the known gaps listed below. We have not had an independent accessibility audit.

How we test

  • Automated checks with axe-core (WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A/AA rules) on every page in our browser test suite, run on every build.
  • Scripted keyboard-only checks: a full game session is played with the keyboard alone in the game's accessible mode, and we check that focus lands where it should and that touch targets are large enough.
  • Colour contrast: our text colours are calculated at 6.4:1 to 14.9:1 against the background, and axe checks every rendered page.

We have not yet tested with screen-reader users or other assistive-technology users.

What is in place

  • Picture login for children who cannot read yet; a read-aloud button beside every student prompt (using voices built into the device); no typing needed when the class QR code is used.
  • An easier-to-read font option (Lexend, with extra spacing) on every student and teacher page.
  • Every game has a keyboard-operable, non-canvas mode presenting the same task; nothing requires dragging.
  • No child fails because of a timer; teachers can give a child up to three times the session length.
  • Visible focus on every control; controls at least 44 by 44 pixels; reduced motion respected.

Known gaps

  • No manual screen-reader pass yet (VoiceOver on iPad, ChromeVox on Chromebook). This is required before a pilot.
  • The picture (canvas) mode of the sample game has no keyboard path of its own; the accessible mode, one tap away, is the keyboard path.
  • Read-aloud depends on the voices installed on the device; some devices have none, and iPads need a tap before sound plays. Every spoken prompt is also shown as text. Recorded human-voice prompts are planned.
  • Login pictures and secret pictures are emoji, which look different on different devices and need text labels for screen readers (they have them, but they have not been tested with screen-reader users).
  • The teacher progress table scrolls sideways on narrow screens.
  • There is no French-language interface.

How we fix things

Accessibility criteria are part of our definition of done: a game cannot be switched on without its verified accessible mode, and automated accessibility checks run on every build.
